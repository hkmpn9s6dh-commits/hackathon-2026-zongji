**What it is.** A 303-second black-and-white film about the last night of a county's manual
telephone exchange in northern China, 1987. One operator, one board, one call that can no longer
be connected. The film takes the festival theme literally: the line is cut, so the human walks.

**Why this story.** The obvious readings of (Dis)Connection are loneliness, screens, the machine
that stops listening. I wanted the version that already happened: the evening a technology that
employed people to *care* — to remember who was where, to say "one moment", to put a child through
to his father — was replaced by one that only switches. Nobody in the film is against the new
exchange. That is what makes it sad.

**How it was made.** Pre-production: screenplay, dialogue and the 23-shot board drafted with a
generative language model, then rewritten by hand. Production: 21 stills generated with an image
model, all composed as 35mm black-and-white film stills, then cut with slow pushes and small pans
only — the source frames are portrait, so there is no room to pan sideways. Post-production: voice
generation with neural TTS for five characters, all music and sound effects synthesized from
scratch (the film's tonic is the 450 Hz dial tone), grain and snow generated per frame, subtitles
derived from the same script file and burned in. No stock footage, no purchased music, no live
action.

**One formal rule.** The film is monochrome except for a single red paper on a door — the only
colour in 303 seconds. It marks the one address the message has to reach.
