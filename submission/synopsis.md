**Synopsis.** Winter, 1987. At eight the next morning the county's manual exchange is cut over to
automatic. Xiulan, forty-one, has worked the board for nineteen years. A lineman brings the
transfer form and tells her: the board gets pulled out, and she goes downstairs to the counter —
sit, take the money.

Near midnight a man calls from a train. He wants his mother told that he cannot make it home
tonight, that she should not wait supper. The line is already gone; the jacks do not light. She
tries again. Nothing.

So she writes the message down, walks out into the snow, past the new concrete exchange with its
one lit window, down a lane to a door with red paper pasted upside down — and says the message
through the door, because that is what an exchange does. The door opens. An old woman asks the only
question that matters: *has he eaten?*

Xiulan hands over what is still warm, refuses to come in — she is not off duty yet — and walks
back. In front of the humming new building she puts her headset on. "Hello. Exchange." The board
behind her is dark.
